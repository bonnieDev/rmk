Try it. Nothing here is sent anywhere.
  1. Motor: shake your pointer near the form, or press “Simulate a tremor.” Buttons get easier to hit, and a bar explains what changed.
  2. Cognitive: click into the long question and stop typing for a few seconds. Bloom offers an easy form, then asks if you’d like a call or your doctor’s help. That request goes with the form.
  3. Break: stall again, in any field, and Bloom offers a break. Press Pause, reload the page, and it offers to pick up right where you were.
  4. Keyboard: put the mouse down and press Tab a few times. Bloom switches to keyboard mode and describes each control.
  5. Text size: use A · A+ · A++ at the top of the form, or act like you’re hunting for it: click the gray space beside the form a few times, or type when you’re not in a field. Bloom makes the text larger for you.
  6. Fill-in: let your browser autofill your name, email and phone, or type a phone number as plain digits and move on. Bloom shows it as 555-123-4567 so it’s easy to check.
  7. Wrong clicks: click “See the eligibility rules” (it isn’t a link), or just miss the Submit button. It all lands in the friction report.

This sample form has four deliberate accessibility mistakes for Bloom to find. See “Tab-ready check.”
