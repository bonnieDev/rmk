# Aether Command & Ghost Crown

*Mission statement. Source copy for rmk.systems, release notes, and anywhere
the question "why is she doing this" needs an answer.*

---

I map human ancestry. Thousands of ancient DNA samples — their coordinates,
their dates, their burials, what was buried with them — on a globe you move
through time. Scrub the timeline and the visible set changes, because the
visible set *is* the queried set.

## The origin

In eighth grade I wrote a paper on Egyptian mummification and was sat down and
made to explain myself. Why was I so obsessed with death?

It wasn't about death. It was about the things they believed fiercely, and what
those beliefs compelled them to build and to do. Mummification isn't morbid —
it's belief producing pyramids, advancing embalming science, and driving a
continental trade in resin and natron. Belief is the archaeology.

That started earlier, in the quiet hours as a latchkey kid, pulling down every
Greek, Roman and Egyptian mythology book I could find. Encyclopedias are
cross-referenced: a god sends you to a region, a region to a period, a period to
a building. It gave me a space in my mind to put things.

That's the whole design. I built what I was already doing.

## What I'm claiming, and what I'm not

This is science and history. It is for everyone, of any belief or none.

I'm not making a case for anyone's beliefs. I'm making the case that beliefs are
evidence — the same case archaeology and anthropology have made for a century.

What a people feared, and what they thought happened after death, is why the
pyramids exist, why the trade routes ran where they ran, and why anyone crossed
an ocean. Treat every belief system as data about human motivation and the
material record becomes readable. Leave it out and history is a list of dates
nobody has a reason to care about.

## What it does

One gesture moves everything. The same scrub through time changes the samples on
the globe, the climate beneath them, the narration, and the score. You watch the
Sahara turn green and go back to desert. You watch the ice retreat and the
routes open.

Nobody has to be told that people moved because they had to. You can see it
happen.

## Why there's music

Data can tell you that a population left. It cannot tell you what leaving felt
like, and that gap is the whole problem — it's the difference between
understanding a fact and imagining the gravity of someone else's mind.

So the map is scored. Every track belongs to a time and, where it belongs to a
people rather than a period, a place. Nobody chooses them: where you are on the
timeline and where you are on the globe select the track, and the player
crossfades. At 800 CE the Rhine, Baghdad and the Orkhon each get a different
one, because they were three different worlds happening at once.

The tracks are made with generative audio tools, directed and edited by me. I'd
rather say that plainly than have it discovered. What's being claimed here is
the direction and the architecture, not the notes.

## The point

Everyone has ancestors in this data. Nobody is outside it.
